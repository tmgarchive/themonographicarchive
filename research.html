Monographic Research Dossiers

Peer-reviewed curatorial papers, material analysis monographs, and archival studies contextualizing post-war tactile abstraction, conceptual linguistics, and participatory narrative repositories. Items indexed reflect physical archival deposits (1996–2011) and digitized open-access transcripts formatted in accordance with the Chicago Manual of Style (17th ed.) and Getty AAT standards.

DOSSIER REF: MAR-PUB-1996.01

Substrate as Monument: Material Density and Matter Painting in Post-War Europe

Citation: Archival Directorate Press. "Substrate as Monument: Material Density and Matter Painting in Post-War Europe." Monographic Research Series 1, no. 1 (1996): 12–34.

Physical substrate analysis of post-war European canvas structures, focusing on inert mineral additions, marble dust aggregate, and raw flax binder in the studio practices of Antoni Tàpies and Alberto Burri. Examines the structural transformation of the support plane from pictorial ground to load-bearing tactile relief.

Keywords (Getty AAT): Tàpies, Burri, Dubuffet, Matter Painting, tactile canvas, impasto relief Status: [PHYSICAL ARCHIVE DEPOSIT — VAULT B-02]
DOSSIER REF: MAR-PUB-1999.03

The Grapheme and the Void: Non-Phonetic Notation and Asemic Script

Citation: Curatorial Research Register. "The Grapheme and the Void: Non-Phonetic Notation and Asemic Script." Journal of Conceptual Epistemology 4, no. 2 (1999): 78–95.

Monographic survey evaluating non-phonetic graphemes, erased calligraphic notation, and sub-linguistic mark-making in mid-century gestural canvas practice. Tracks the decoupling of scribal gesture from formal syntax within post-1950 European and North American conceptual registers.

Keywords (Getty AAT): Twombly, Michaux, Kosuth, Conceptual Semiosis, asemic script, non-phonetic notation Status: [PHYSICAL ARCHIVE DEPOSIT — VAULT A-07]
DOSSIER REF: MAR-PUB-2004.02

Temporal Seriality: Numerical Units, Duration Registers, and Monocular Marking

Citation: Universal Index Proceedings. "Temporal Seriality: Numerical Units, Duration Registers, and Monocular Marking." Monographic Survey Proceedings 9, no. 3 (2004): 110–132.

Taxonomic review of serial counting systems, date registers, and chronological notation in post-war conceptualism. Focuses on Roman Opałka’s monochrome numerical progression (1–∞) and Hanne Darboven’s calendar transcription structures as systematic methodologies for mapping human duration onto physical canvas supports.

Keywords (Getty AAT): Opałka, Darboven, Boetti, Serial Duration, numerical units, time-based registers Status: [PHYSICAL ARCHIVE DEPOSIT — VAULT C-01]
DOSSIER REF: MAR-PUB-2011.05

Squeegee, Strata, and Erasure: Mechanized Gesture in Post-Industrial Abstraction

Citation: Monographic Studies Press. "Squeegee, Strata, and Erasure: Mechanized Gesture in Post-Industrial Abstraction." European Art Registry Studies 16, no. 1 (2011): 45–67.

Technical evaluation of mechanical pigment application, squeegee traction, and erasure dynamics in late 20th-century process abstraction. Analyzes layer stripping and industrial surface friction in the studio methodologies of Gerhard Richter and Christopher Wool, documenting structural paint accumulation over multi-stage production sequences.

Keywords (Getty AAT): Richter, Wool, Mehretu, Process Abstraction, squeegee application, layer stripping Status: [PHYSICAL ARCHIVE DEPOSIT — VAULT C-12]
DOSSIER REF: MAR-PUB-2018.04

Participatory Provenance: Institutional Word Gathering and Circular Substrates

Citation: Malik, Hesham. "Participatory Provenance: Institutional Word Gathering and Circular Substrates." International Archival Papers in Art History 14, no. 2 (2018): 45–68.

Curatorial dossier documenting multi-institutional narrative gathering and recycled paper fiber substrates within The Words Project and Life of a Box archive series (Malik). Details the technical integration of donated linguistic units and circular paper materials—sourced from international academic, environmental, and sovereign entities—into heavy mineral pigment layers to create permanent participatory registers.
