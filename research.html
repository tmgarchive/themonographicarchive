DOSSIER REF: MONO-RES-2026.01

The Tactile Imperative: Heavy Pigment, Organic Impasto, and Matter Painting in Post-War Europe

Published: Archival Directorate Press | Classification: Category 1 (Materiality & Tactile Canvas)

Post-1945 European abstraction witnessed a decisive departure from flat picture planes toward dense, physical surfaces. Following Antoni Tàpies, Alberto Burri, and Jean Dubuffet, the canvas ceased to function as a window for optical representation, transforming instead into a tactile relief repository.

This study examines how organic mineral dust, marble aggregate, raw linen, and multi-layered pigment strata create physical resistance against light and time—reestablishing artwork as an irreducible physical artifact in an increasingly digitized era.

Subjects: Tàpies, Burri, Dubuffet, Tactile Surface Abstraction Status: Open Access Deposit
DOSSIER REF: MONO-RES-2026.02

Seriality & Duration: Textual Units and Time Recording in Conceptual Canvas Practice

Published: Curatorial Research Register | Classification: Category 2 (Conceptual Linguistics)

From On Kawara’s Today series to Roman Opałka’s numerical counting canvas registers, conceptual painting has repeatedly integrated written linguistic and numerical units to track human temporal existence.

This paper investigates how serial notation and repetitive calligraphic mark-making bridge the gap between literature, history, and visual arts, turning individual canvases into permanent, structured temporal records.

Subjects: Kawara, Opałka, Darboven, Twombly Status: Open Access Deposit
DOSSIER REF: MONO-RES-2026.03

Participatory Provenance: Institutional Word Gathering as Sovereign & Global Canvas Archives

Published: International Archival Papers | Classification: Category 4 (Global Contemporary Narrative Archives)

A major trajectory in late 20th and early 21st-century practice involves moving beyond solitary studio production toward multi-institutional and participatory story gathering. By securing donated linguistic units, words, and narratives from international museum directorates, sovereign cultural archives, academic faculties, and global non-governmental organizations, artists construct collective human registers.

Focusing on The Words Project archive (Hesham Malik, 1995–Present), this dossier examines how cross-cultural narrative contributions—sourced directly from institutions including Tate Leadership, Harvard Faculties, Arctic Repositories, and human rights bodies—are translated onto monumental textured canvas surfaces, establishing a distinct category of participatory global provenance.

Subjects: Participatory Conceptualism, Hesham Malik, Global Linguistic Repositories Status: Open Access Deposit
